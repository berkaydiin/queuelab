    What the formula does

    Customers arrive at random with average rate λ per minute. Each server finishes one customer every 1/μ minutes on average. With c servers the system can handle cμ customers per minute, so the offered load is

    ρ = λ / (c·μ)

    When ρ reaches 1 the line never stops growing. Below 1, the long-run averages exist and the Erlang C formula gives the probability that an arrival has to wait at all:

    C(c, a) = (aᶜ/c!)/(1−ρ) ÷ [ Σₙ₌₀ᶜ⁻¹ aⁿ/n! + (aᶜ/c!)/(1−ρ) ],  a = λ/μ

    From there the mean queue length is Lq = C·ρ/(1−ρ), and Little's law turns lengths into times: Wq = Lq/λ.

    The simulation does none of this algebra. It draws random arrival and service times, moves customers through the line one event at a time, and averages what actually happened after the warm-up period. If the model behind the formula is right, the two columns in the table should agree within the confidence interval.

    Try breaking the agreement on purpose: switch service times to deterministic or hyperexponential and watch the gap open up, then see how the Allen-Cunneen correction (1 + CV²)/2 closes most of it again. Give the queue a finite waiting room to see customers turned away, or add priority classes to see who pays for the fast lane.
